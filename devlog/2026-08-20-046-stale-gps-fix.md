# 046 · The vehicle kept transmitting a fix it had already lost

**Date** 2026-08-20
**Type** fix
**Refs** ISS-14

## What

The GPS pin fix from entry 042 worked — `20260819-234649-serial.log` carries real
coordinates for Cyberjaya and a satellite count that moves. It also exposed a fault that
could not appear while the GPS was mute.

At the end of that log the satellite count decays and the position does not:

| seq | sat | lat / lng / spd |
|---|---|---|
| 234 | 9 | tracking normally |
| 235–236 | 8 | |
| 237–238 | 2 | |
| **239–252** | **0** | **`2.92717, 101.76009, 1.6` — identical for 14 packets** |

The fix was lost, `sat` reported that honestly, and latitude, longitude and speed froze at
their last values and kept transmitting as though live.

## Cause

`Sensors.ino` gated on `gps.location.isValid()`. In TinyGPSPlus that flag latches true on
the first successful parse and **never returns to false** — it means *"has ever been
valid"*, not *"is valid now"*. Freshness is a separate `age()` call, which nothing used.
`gps.speed` carries the same latch, which is why speed froze at 1.6 km/h alongside it.

The comment already sitting above those lines is the part worth keeping in view:

> *"An invalid fix reports 0.0 rather than a stale position. The dashboard treats exact
> zeros as no fix — a stale one would be plotted as real."*

The intent was exactly right. The API did not deliver it, and nothing checked.

## Why it mattered

`hasFix()` was `lat !== 0 || lng !== 0`, so the dashboard saw a fix and printed a confident
coordinate. `fixQuality(0, true)` returned **"Poor"** — a warning tone that reads as *a fix
with bad geometry*, not *this position is stale and the receiver has none*.

On descent that is the recovery position. It could be minutes old with nothing on screen
saying so, and it is the coordinate somebody would drive to.

## Fixed at both ends

**Firmware** — `Sensors.ino` now gates on age, with `GPS_FIX_MAX_AGE_MS 3000` in
`Config.h`. Three cycles, not one: the GPS updates at 1 Hz and the vehicle samples at 1 Hz
with no phase relationship between them, so age at sample time is routinely several hundred
ms on a perfectly healthy fix. A threshold near 1000 would blank the position at random.
Speed gets the same check.

**Dashboard** — `lib/geo.ts` gains `fixStale()` and `hasLiveFix()`. `hasFix()` is unchanged
and stays a pure statement about the coordinates; the new pair are statements about whether
to believe them. `sat === 0` alongside a non-zero position is a contradiction — the vehicle
claiming a fix while reporting it is tracking nothing.

- `GpsPanel` — a stale fix is now **alert** tone, labelled `Stale — 0 satellites`, and the
  coordinates are withheld rather than shown. A stale position is worse than no position:
  it is wrong *and* it looks right.
- `GroundTrackPanel` — the trace and the drift figure filter on `hasLiveFix`. A repeating
  coordinate would otherwise pin the last point somewhere the vehicle had left and bias the
  distance-from-launch reading toward it.

Both ends deliberately, not one. The firmware fix stops the vehicle asserting something
false; the dashboard fix means a vehicle that still does it cannot fool the ground — which
also makes every capture already on disk readable, since the corpus predates the flash.

## Result

`Sensors.ino`, `Config.h`, `lib/geo.ts`, `panels/GpsPanel.tsx`,
`panels/GroundTrackPanel.tsx`.

**Tests: 118 backend, 70 frontend** (was 66). Four new, all built from the measured log
rather than invented: a position with zero satellites is stale, a real fix is not, `0,0`
with zero satellites is **not** stale — that is the firmware honestly reporting nothing —
and a stale frame passes `hasFix` while failing `hasLiveFix`.

Firmware not compiled or flashed; there is no toolchain here.

**Surfaced, not fixed.** The vehicle reads three of the four GPS quality signals available
to it and sends the weakest one:

| signal | where | status |
|---|---|---|
| GGA fix quality (0/1/2) | GGA field 6 | authoritative — **not read** |
| HDOP | GGA field 8, `gps.hdop` | actual accuracy — **not read** |
| satellite count | `gps.satellites` | proxy — read and sent |
| `lat`/`lng` ≠ 0,0 | inferred | weakest — what the dashboard used |

Reading GGA fix quality would have made this bug impossible rather than detectable. It
costs nothing in airtime, since it is a decision the vehicle can make locally before
choosing what to put in the packet.
