# 035 · 3D pose model, on canvas

**Date** 2026-08-19
**Type** change
**Refs** ISS-08

Supersedes the revert in entry 014, on Aiman's decision and with new information.

## What

An eased 3D model of the CanSat, as a second representation inside the Attitude panel.
A `Model / Horizon` toggle in the panel header switches between them; both show the same
numbers.

Created:

- `frontend/src/lib/pose.ts` — projection maths, pure
- `frontend/src/components/PoseView.tsx` — canvas renderer with the animation loop
- `frontend/src/lib/__tests__/pose.test.ts` — 15 tests

Edited: `panels/AttitudePanel.tsx`, `styles/panels.css`.

The model is a plain cylinder for now, at Aiman's request — a real body will replace it.

## Why

Entry 014 reverted the Three.js attitude and asked that 3D not be re-proposed **without
new information**. There is new information, and it is not "we want it now":

**The revert's stated objection was practicality, not desirability** — *"a WebGL
dependency, a model pipeline, and a placeholder to maintain"*, for 733 kB against a
215 kB bundle. A CanSat is a cylinder, and an orthographic projection of a cylinder is
arithmetic. Painter's algorithm is exact for a convex solid, so a depth buffer buys
nothing here. **Bundle cost: 4 kB.** The objection was to the tool, not to the idea.

Three decisions inside it, each removing an ambiguity that would otherwise mislead:

- **The body is not rotated by yaw.** Yaw is integrated from the gyro and drifts without
  bound; a model quietly spinning on a still bench would be the most misleading thing on
  the screen. Yaw is a tick on a dashed ring *outside* the model, visibly not part of the
  solid — so an estimate can never be read as a measurement.
- **A longitudinal stripe**, because a bare cylinder is rotationally symmetric about its
  own axis and roll would otherwise be invisible — the model would sit still through the
  motion it most needs to show.
- **Light nose, dark tail.** An inverted cylinder has the same silhouette as an upright
  one. Different-coloured ends are the only cue that separates them, and on a descending
  CanSat that is the distinction worth seeing. Added after the first render, where a
  vehicle at roll 160° looked upright.

**Easing is presentation, not data.** Telemetry lands at 1 Hz and a model that snapped to
each sample would jump once a second and read as broken. The interpolation is exponential
and framerate-independent, so it eases at the same speed on any machine. It **lags the
telemetry by about 0.22 s**, and between samples it is showing an interpolation rather
than a measurement — acceptable for a shape read at a glance, wrong for a number, which is
why the readouts beside it stay raw and unsmoothed.

Angles interpolate by shortest arc. Without that, a reading crossing 350° → 10° sweeps
340° the wrong way round, and the model spins backwards every time it passes the wrap
point.

## Result

Verified in the browser against the mock, across the states that matter:

- Upright on the pad, coloured, nose up
- Tilted at pitch −33° / roll −30°, leaning correctly
- **At roll 165°, showing the dark tail cap face-on** — the inversion cue working
- Greyed out under `FREEFALL` and `UNDER ACCELERATION`, so an unreliable pose does not
  look like a confident one
- Toggle to Horizon and back, both redrawing immediately

180 tests pass — 118 backend, 62 frontend (15 new). Bundle 226 kB / 79.5 kB, up 4 kB from
the 2D-only build. For comparison, the Three.js version was 733 kB / 207 kB.

Left open:

- `wiki/decisions/frontend.md` still records the 3D revert as standing, with a note that
  its precondition was "partially met, and not enough". That is now out of date in one
  direction — the rendering objection is gone, the drifting-yaw one is not, and the model
  answers it by not rendering yaw at all. Worth an amendment, which is its own decision.
- The cylinder is a placeholder. Replacing it means editing `cylinderMesh()` to emit a
  different face list; nothing else in the renderer knows what shape it is drawing.
