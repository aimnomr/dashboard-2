## What

Step 2 of `wiki/decisions/dashboard-gen3-plan.md`, and the last thing standing between

The envelope keeps the flat shape the plan specified — `expected`, `received`, `lost`,
`loss_pct` — and adds `rolling`, plus `crc_failed`, `duplicates`, `restarts`,
`baseline_seq` and `last_seq`.

`Pipeline._envelope` became `Pipeline._messages`, returning a list, because one raw line
can now produce two messages.

## Why

The module is separate and pure because **no capture contains a reboot, a duplicate or a
dropout**. Every awkward case has to be constructed, and constructing them inside the
pipeline would mean a socket and an event loop per test.

Rules implemented, each because the obvious version produces a specific wrong answer:

| Rule | Wrong answer avoided |
|---|---|
| S1 · CRC-valid frames only | A corrupted `seq` invents a gap or a restart — when the link is worst |
| S2 · Baseline is the first `seq` seen | Starting mid-flight at 412 opens by reporting 411 lost |
| S3 · Rolling *and* session | One cumulative figure hides a collapse behind twenty good minutes |
| S4 · Backwards = restart | Naive arithmetic reports 1.8 M lost the instant the vehicle reboots |
| S5 · No counter → null | 0% is a fabricated measurement, the same failure as deriving loss from `rx_index` |

**Loss is computed in the backend, and that is the point.** A browser may connect late or
reconnect mid-flight with a capped history buffer. Computed client-side, two operators
would read different numbers off the same flight.

**A restart resets the loss counters but not the diagnostics.** `crc_failed`, `duplicates`
and `restarts` survive, because zeroing them on a reboot destroys the evidence at the
exact moment someone is working out what went wrong. The plan said "reset the counters";
this is a narrower reading of which ones, recorded here as a deliberate choice.

**The restart is announced before the frame that revealed it.** Sent the other way round,
the chart draws a segment across the discontinuity before being told there was one.

## Result

Verified live, against a `--loop` replay at ×50 — which is currently the only source that
can produce a restart at all:

- The probe **joined mid-flight at seq 56 and read 0% loss, not 55 lost** (S2)
- `vehicle_restart: seq 227 → 1`, and the message order around it was
  `['frame', 'restart', 'frame']`
- After the restart: `baseline_seq: 1`, `restarts: 1`, loss back to 0%

Both S5 branches confirmed in the browser: GEN3 replay reads **`LOSS (60) · 0.0% ·
session 0.0% · 0 lost`**; the GEN2 mock reads **`LOSS · n/a · no counter`** in neutral
grey. No console errors.

150 tests pass — 103 backend (27 new), 47 frontend (4 new). Bundle 221.8 kB / 77.9 kB.

Left open, and worth knowing:

- **Nothing has yet shown a non-zero loss figure.** Both captures are clean and the mock
  is GEN2, so the escalating tones are covered by unit tests but have never been seen on
  screen. Step 4 — the GEN3 mock with injectable `seq` gaps and CRC failures — is what
  fixes that, and it is the difference between testing the loss display and testing it
  *on launch day*.
- **S4 treats any backwards `seq` as a reboot**, exactly as specified. A backwards jump of
  three would therefore read as a restart, though a vehicle cannot reboot and reach 1799
  instantly. LoRa does not reorder, so this has no known trigger — but the rule is wider
  than the situation it describes, and a sanity bound on the jump size would close it.
  Not changed here: the rule is ratified, and narrowing it is a decision.
- `lib/link.ts` still renders the chute as **"Deployed"**, which S8 forbids. Still step 6,
  still unbuilt, now recorded in three places.
